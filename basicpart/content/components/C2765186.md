## What it is

A right-angle USB Type-C receptacle with 16 contacts, rated for 3 A. Like the
other Type-C part in the curated picks it omits the high-speed pairs, keeping
power, USB 2.0 data, the CC pins and sideband. The right-angle body is the usual
choice for a board edge, where the cable enters horizontally. [1]

The curated record carries a specific warning worth repeating: this *is* a
16-contact connector, not one of the six-pin power-only Type-C receptacles that
look superficially similar and are much cheaper. The two have entirely different
footprints. [1]

## What can be said

**The repository's own warning is the key content**: "This is a 16-contact
connector, not a six-pin power-only receptacle. Verify the footprint and pin
map." Six-pin Type-C receptacles are common and cheap, and they are frequently
confused with 16-pin ones in library files. Getting this wrong wastes a board
spin. [1]

**3 A needs the CC resistors to be right.** A device drawing 3 A from a Type-C
source must negotiate for it. At minimum that means correct 5.1 kΩ pull-downs on
CC1 and CC2; for anything above 5 V it means a Power Delivery controller.

**Right-angle connectors take the strain.** A cable pulled sideways levers on the
solder joints. Use a footprint with through-hole retention tabs if the product
will be handled.

## Watch out for

- **Verify pin count and footprint** against the manufacturer's drawing.
- **Two 5.1 kΩ CC pull-downs**, one on each CC pin.
- **Mechanical retention matters** on a right-angle part.
- **Extended-tier assembly costs more** and may be restricted for connectors.
