# SS14

## What it is

The SS14 is the workhorse Schottky rectifier for small power circuits: 1 A of
average current, 40 V of reverse voltage, in the SMA (DO-214AC) package. Its job
is to conduct with as little voltage drop as possible and to stop conducting the
instant the current reverses. [1]

Its most common use is as the catch diode in a non-synchronous buck converter —
the part that carries the inductor current while the switch is off. The TPS5430,
TPS54331, LM2596 and XL1509 in this catalog all need one. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Function | Single Schottky barrier rectifier [1] | Standalone diode; no pairs or bridges. |
| Repetitive peak reverse voltage | 40 V [1] | Enough for a 12 V converter with margin; not for 24 V, where the SS24 or SS34 is the right choice. |
| Average forward current | 1 A rectified; 30 A non-repetitive peak surge [1] | The surge rating matters at startup, when the output capacitor charges. |
| Forward voltage | 550 mV at 1 A [1] | Roughly half a silicon diode's drop. In a 5 V converter at 1 A that is 550 mW saved compared with a 1N4007. |
| Recovery or capacitance | Schottky diodes have essentially no reverse-recovery charge, which is what makes them suitable for switching converters | This is the property that a fast-recovery silicon diode such as the US1M cannot match. |
| Operating temperature | Junction −55 °C to +150 °C [1] | Wide. |

## What the datasheet actually says

The catalog record gives a reverse leakage current of **200 µA at 40 V** — three
orders of magnitude more than a silicon diode, and the fundamental Schottky
trade-off. In a buck converter that leakage is irrelevant. In a battery isolation
or reverse-protection circuit it is a continuous drain, and it rises sharply with
temperature. [1]

The 550 mV forward drop is quoted at the full 1 A. At lower currents it is lower,
which is why the SS14 is efficient across a converter's load range. [1]

## Watch out for

- **40 V is not much.** A 24 V buck converter's switch node swings to −0.5 V and
  up to 24 V plus ringing; 40 V leaves little margin. Use a higher-voltage
  Schottky.
- **Leakage matters in low-power circuits.** For battery isolation, consider a
  MOSFET-based ideal diode instead.
- **1 A is an average rating** with the SMA package soldered to a reasonable
  copper area. Check the dissipation: 550 mV × 1 A is 550 mW.
- **Place it close to the switch node.** In a converter, the loop formed by the
  input capacitor, the switch and this diode should be as small as possible.
