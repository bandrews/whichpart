## What it is

A white surface-mount LED in the same 0603 package as the red one. White LEDs
are made by putting a yellow phosphor over a blue die, which is why the catalog
records the lens colour as yellow — that is the phosphor you can see when the LED
is off. [1]

It is brighter than the red LED at a much smaller current, but it needs a
noticeably higher forward voltage, which rules it out of low-voltage designs. [1]

## Watch out for

- **Always fit a series resistor.** An LED has no internal current limit; connect
  one directly across a supply and it destroys itself.
- **Forward voltage varies part to part** and falls as the LED warms up, which is
  why current, not voltage, is what you control.
- **You almost certainly do not want the full rated current.** A few milliamps is
  plenty for an indicator, and it saves power and extends life.
- **Check the polarity mark** in your footprint against the manufacturer's
  drawing — 0603 LED cathode markings are not standardised across vendors.
