## What it is

A red surface-mount LED in the 0603 package — 1.6 × 0.8 mm, the size of a grain of coarse salt. It
is the standard "power on" or "activity" indicator, and at well under a cent
each there is no reason to be sparing with them. [1]

Red is the easiest colour to drive: its forward voltage is low enough that even a
1.8 V supply can light it, and it is the most efficient of the common indicator
colours, so it looks bright at very small currents. [1]

## What the specification implies

**The forward voltage is a range, not a value.** The catalog records 1.8 V to
2.4 V — a 600 mV spread between one part and the next. With a fixed series
resistor from a 3.3 V rail, that spread alone changes the current by roughly a
factor of two between the extremes, so two LEDs from the same reel can look
visibly different. Running well below the rated current makes this worse, not
better, because the resistor drop is smaller. If you need matched brightness
across several indicators, drive them from a current source or accept the
variation. [1]

**Peak and dominant wavelength are different numbers.** The catalog gives a peak
of 645 nm and a dominant wavelength of 615–630 nm. The peak is where the chip
emits most strongly; the dominant wavelength is the pure colour a person would
say it matches. For an indicator only the second matters, and 615–630 nm is a
slightly orange red. [1]

**300 mcd at 20 mA is bright for an indicator.** At 2 mA — a tenth of the rating
— you still get roughly 30 mcd, which is comfortably visible indoors and draws a
tenth of the power. There is rarely a reason to run a panel LED at its rating. [1]

## Watch out for

- **Always fit a series resistor.** An LED has no internal current limit; connect
  one directly across a supply and it destroys itself.
- **Forward voltage varies part to part** and falls as the LED warms up, which is
  why current, not voltage, is what you control.
- **You almost certainly do not want the full rated current.** A few milliamps is
  plenty for an indicator, and it saves power and extends life.
- **Check the polarity mark** in your footprint against the manufacturer's
  drawing — 0603 LED cathode markings are not standardised across vendors.
