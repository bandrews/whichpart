# BAV99W

## What it is

BAV99 is one of the most common small-signal diode types in electronics: two fast
silicon switching diodes inside one three-pin package, connected in series. The
datasheet's function diagram shows the arrangement — pin 1 is an anode, pin 2 is
a cathode, and pin 3 is the node where the first diode's cathode meets the second
diode's anode. Bring signals to whichever pins suit you and the same part becomes
a clamp to two supply rails, two independent diodes, or a series pair. The `W`
suffix means the small SOT-323 (SC-70) package. [1]

The manufacturer, Zhuhai Hongjiacheng, lists the intended uses as small-signal
switching and ultra-high-speed switching. [1] In everyday designs that means
clamping an input pin to the supply rails, steering a signal, protecting a MOSFET
gate, or building a small logic OR gate out of two diodes.

## What the datasheet actually says

**The reverse-voltage rating contradicts itself, and 75 V is the safe reading.**
The maximum-ratings table lists a repetitive peak reverse voltage of 100 V. Three
other statements in the same document point at 75 V: the maximum RMS voltage is
75 V, reverse breakdown is specified as 75 V *minimum* at 100 µA, and the summary
box on the front page reads "Reverse Voltage 75 V". A part cannot sensibly be
rated to withstand 100 V when the manufacturer only promises it will not break
down below 75 V — and the leakage specification stops at 75 V too (2.5 µA there,
and 25 nA at 25 V), so above 75 V nothing at all is characterised. Design to
75 V. That also settles a question this repository had previously left open,
since the catalog's 75 V turns out to match the manufacturer's own front page.
[1] [2]

**Leakage grows a hundredfold over a threefold change in voltage.** The
guaranteed maximum reverse current is 25 nA at 25 V but 2.5 µA at 75 V. If you
are clamping a high-impedance node — a sensor output, a sample-and-hold, a
long-running RC timer — the bias across the diode is what decides whether the
leakage is negligible or is quietly dominating your circuit. [1]

**200 mW is close to what a fully loaded diode actually dissipates.** At the
rated 150 mA the maximum forward drop is 1.25 V, which is 188 mW — essentially
the entire budget. The derating curve (Fig. 4) starts falling from 200 mW at
25 °C ambient and reaches zero at 150 °C, and the typical thermal resistance is
625 °C/W from junction to ambient, so 200 mW alone lifts the junction about
125 °C. Treat 150 mA as a short-term figure, not a continuous one in warm
air. [1]

## Watch out for

- **Design to 75 V**, not to the 100 V in the maximum-ratings table, and not to
  the 100 V you will find on other manufacturers' BAV99W datasheets. [1] [3]
- **Pin 3 is the common connection, and it is the single pin on the opposite side
  of the package** from pins 1 and 2 — not a middle pin in a row of three. Check
  the pinout against your circuit; common-anode and common-cathode members of
  this family exist and look identical on a board. [1]
- **200 mW is a package rating shared by both diodes.** If both are working hard
  at once, budget accordingly. [1]
- **1.25 V of forward drop at 150 mA** makes this unsuitable as a power
  rectifier. [1]
