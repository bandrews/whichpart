## What it is

A USB Type-C receptacle — the connector, not the electronics — with 16
contacts, made by Jing Extension (Jing Extension of the Electronic Co.). The
full Type-C specification defines 24 contacts; 16-contact parts omit the
high-speed pairs that USB 3 and alternate modes need, keeping power delivery,
USB 2.0 data, the two CC pins and the sideband pins. [1]

For a board that only needs USB 2.0 and power — which is most microcontroller
boards — a 16-contact receptacle is the right choice: fewer pins to route, fewer
to solder, and the same physical connector the user plugs into. [1] [2]

| Contact count | 16 [1] | Not the full 24. USB 3 SuperSpeed and DisplayPort alternate mode are not possible, whatever the listing's "USB 3.1" label suggests — see below. |

| Voltage rating | Not stated on the listing [1] | — |

## What the specification implies

**The LCSC listing labels it "USB 3.1", which a 16-pin connector cannot fully
deliver.** USB 3.x SuperSpeed needs the four high-speed differential pairs that
only exist on 24-contact receptacles; a 16-position part carries USB 2.0 data
plus power and CC. Treat the label as a family designation, not a capability. [1]

**Stock was 577 units at $0.61 in ones** when checked — thin for a connector,
and worth confirming before committing a design. [1]

**The footprint is the whole risk, and the repository already warns about it.**
The curated record's note reads: "Ordinary Extended part; verify its exact
footprint and JLCPCB assembly restrictions before use." USB-C receptacles vary
in shell-tab position, board cut-out, and mounting depth; a wrong footprint is
not a routing error you can patch. [2]

**Do not forget the CC resistors.** A USB-C device that wants to draw power must
present 5.1 kΩ pull-downs on CC1 and CC2 (two separate resistors, not one). No
connector does this for you.

## Watch out for

- **Verify the footprint against the manufacturer's drawing**, not against
  another board's library.
- **Thin stock (577 at last check).** The other Type-C pick (C2765186) had over
  a million units.
- **Fit two 5.1 kΩ CC pull-down resistors** on a device-side port.
- **Extended-tier assembly costs more** and may be restricted for connectors.
- **Ignore the "USB 3.1" label** — 16 pins means USB 2.0 data.
