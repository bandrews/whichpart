# 74HC595D,118

## What it is

The 74HC595 is the standard way to get more output pins. You clock eight bits in
one at a time on a serial line, then pulse a second clock to move all eight to the
output pins at once. Three microcontroller pins become eight outputs, and because
there is a serial output pin as well, you can daisy-chain them — three pins can
drive twenty-four outputs, or forty. [1]

The two-clock arrangement is what makes it pleasant to use. The outputs do not
change while you are shifting data in; they update in one clean step when you
pulse the storage clock. That is why it drives LED displays without visible
flicker. [1]

## What the datasheet actually says

**Both clocks are separate, and the order matters.** Data shifts on the
LOW-to-HIGH edge of SHCP, and transfers to the outputs on the LOW-to-HIGH edge of
STCP. Nexperia notes that if you tie both clocks together, the shift register will
always be one clock pulse ahead of the storage register — a legitimate trick that
saves a pin, at the cost of a one-bit offset you must account for. [1]

**Output enable does not affect the registers.** Taking OE high puts the outputs
into high impedance but leaves the stored data intact. That makes OE usable as a
PWM brightness control for an LED array without disturbing the pattern. [1]

**The asynchronous reset (MR) clears the shift register only**, not the storage
register. Data already latched to the outputs stays there until you pulse
STCP. [1]

**Input clamp diodes** let you interface inputs above V<sub>CC</sub> through a
current-limiting resistor. [1]

## Watch out for

- **70 mA total supply current is the ceiling**, not 8 × 35 mA. Lighting eight
  LEDs at 20 mA each exceeds it.
- **Add a series resistor per LED.** The outputs are not current-limited.
- **HC is not HCT.** For 3.3 V control of a 5 V-powered register, use HCT.
- **Decouple properly.** Eight outputs switching together draw a sharp current
  spike; a 100 nF capacitor at each device is not optional in a long chain.
