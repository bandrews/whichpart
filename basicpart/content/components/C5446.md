# XC6206P332MR-G

## What it is

A three-terminal fixed 3.3 V regulator in a SOT-23, delivering up to 200 mA and
drawing about a microamp when idle. Along with the AMS1117 it is one of the two
regulators you see most often on small boards — the difference being that the
XC6206 is designed for battery use and the AMS1117 is not. [1]

The order code decodes: `XC6206` is the family, `P` the package type, `332` the
3.3 V output, `MR` the SOT-23 package, `-G` the lead-free/green designation. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Output voltage | Fixed 3.3 V [1] | The `332` in the part number. The family covers many other voltages with different digits. |
| Output current | 200 mA [1] | A fifth of the AMS1117's rating, but plenty for a microcontroller and a few sensors. |
| Input voltage range | Up to 6 V [1] | **Much lower than the AMS1117's.** This is a 5 V-in part; do not feed it 12 V. |
| Dropout voltage | Not recorded in the catalog [1] | Torex's XC6206 series is specified in the hundreds of millivolts at rated current — get the figure from the datasheet before designing a battery discharge curve. |
| Quiescent current | 1 µA [1] | The reason to choose it. The AMS1117 draws 5 mA — five thousand times more. |
| Output accuracy | Not recorded in the catalog [1] | — |
| Operating temperature | −40 °C to +85 °C ambient [1] | Industrial range. |

## What the specification implies

**1 µA of quiescent current changes what is possible.** A design that sleeps most
of the time and wakes occasionally is dominated by the regulator's standing draw.
At 5 mA an AMS1117 flattens a 2,000 mAh cell in about two weeks doing nothing; at
1 µA the XC6206 would take centuries, and the cell's own self-discharge becomes
the limit.

**6 V maximum input is a hard constraint.** This part is for USB, a single
lithium cell, or four alkalines. A 12 V rail needs a different regulator, or a
pre-regulator.

**200 mA and SOT-23 means thermal limits arrive quickly.** Dropping 5 V to 3.3 V
at 200 mA dissipates 340 mW, which is a lot for a SOT-23 with no thermal pad.
Check the package's dissipation rating against your worst case.

**Over-current and short-circuit protection are listed** in the catalog record,
so a shorted output should not destroy it. [1]

## Watch out for

- **6 V input ceiling.** The most common way to destroy one.
- **Not a drop-in for an AMS1117.** Different pinout family, different current
  rating, and a much lower input limit — though both are SOT-223/SOT-23 style
  three-terminal parts.
- **CMOS LDOs care about the output capacitor.** Follow Torex's recommended value
  and type; substituting a much larger or much smaller one can cause instability.
- **Counterfeits exist.** The XC6206 is widely cloned; the LCSC record naming
  Torex is worth something.
