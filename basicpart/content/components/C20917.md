# AO3400A

## What it is

An N-channel MOSFET in a SOT-23 package, used as a low-side switch: put the load
between the positive supply and the drain, ground the source, and drive the gate
from a microcontroller pin. It handles up to 30 V and several amps. [1]

The reason it is the default rather than one of a hundred similar parts is its
gate behaviour. It is a logic-level device that turns on properly at 2.5 V, so a
3.3 V microcontroller pin drives it fully. Many older SOT-23 MOSFETs need 10 V on
the gate to reach their rated on-resistance. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Channel | N-channel, enhancement mode, trench technology [1] | Low-side switching. For high-side you want a P-channel part such as the AO3401A. |
| Drain-source voltage | 30 V [1] | Comfortable for 12 V loads with margin for inductive spikes; not enough for 24 V motors without care. |
| Continuous drain current | 5.7 A at V<sub>GS</sub> = 10 V [1] | A package rating in a SOT-23 — thermally limited well below this in practice. See below. |
| On-resistance at stated drive | Under 26.5 mΩ at V<sub>GS</sub> = 10 V, under 32 mΩ at 4.5 V, under 48 mΩ at 2.5 V [1] | The 2.5 V figure is the important one: it is what you get from 3.3 V logic, and it is still low. |
| Gate threshold | 0.65 V minimum, 1.05 V typical, 1.45 V maximum at V<sub>DS</sub> = V<sub>GS</sub>, I<sub>D</sub> = 250 mA [1] | Threshold is where it *starts* to conduct, not where it is fully on. Drive the gate to at least 2.5 V. |
| Operating temperature | Junction and storage −55 °C to +150 °C per the catalog record; the datasheet's thermal figures assume T<sub>J(MAX)</sub> = 150 °C [1] [2] | Junction, not ambient. |

## What the datasheet actually says

**The 5.7 A rating depends on your board.** Alpha & Omega footnotes its thermal
figures as measured on a specific copper area — "2 oz. Copper, assuming a maximum
junction temperature of T<sub>J(MAX)</sub> = 150 °C". A SOT-23 with a small pad
will not sustain 5.7 A. Treat one or two amps as realistic for a typical
layout. [1]

**On-state drain current reaches 30 A** at V<sub>GS</sub> = 4.5 V and
V<sub>DS</sub> = 5 V — that is the silicon's capability in a pulse, not a
continuous rating, and it illustrates how far the package rather than the die is
the limit. [1]

**On-resistance rises with temperature.** At T<sub>J</sub> = 125 °C the 10 V
figure goes from 18 mΩ typical to 28 mΩ typical. Since resistance causes the
heating, this is a feedback loop to account for. [1]

## Watch out for

- **Threshold voltage is not turn-on voltage.** At 1.45 V of gate drive it barely
  conducts. Drive it to 3.3 V or more.
- **The package, not the die, limits current.** Budget copper.
- **The body diode is always there** — it conducts from source to drain
  regardless of the gate, which matters if you reverse the load.
- **Gate charge is 7 nC at 10 V**, so PWM at high frequency costs real gate-drive
  current from your microcontroller pin. [2]
