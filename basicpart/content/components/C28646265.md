# BSS138W

> **Note on sources.** The part supplied here is `hongjiacheng`-branded, and that
> manufacturer's datasheet is served only through LCSC, which blocks automated
> retrieval. BSS138W is an industry-standard type, so this note cites the
> JLCPCB/LCSC catalog record as `[1]` for the part actually being supplied, and
> onsemi's BSS138W datasheet as `[2]` for the type — flagging where the two
> differ. See `ISSUES.md`.

## What it is

The BSS138 is a small logic-level N-channel MOSFET. It cannot switch much current
— a couple of hundred milliamps — but that is not usually why it is chosen. Its
fame comes from one specific circuit: the two-resistor, one-MOSFET bidirectional
level shifter that lets a 3.3 V I²C bus talk to a 5 V one. Almost every I²C level
shifter board uses BSS138s. [1]

The `W` suffix is the SOT-323 (SC-70) package, smaller than the original
SOT-23. [1]

## What the datasheet actually says

**The level-shifter circuit relies on the body diode as much as the channel.**
Source to the low-voltage side, drain to the high-voltage side, gate tied to the
low-voltage supply. When the low side pulls down, the MOSFET conducts and pulls
the high side down with it. When the high side pulls down, current flows through
the body diode, which then turns the MOSFET on properly. The circuit is
bidirectional because of that sequence, not despite it.

**Gate threshold matters more than on-resistance here.** For the shifter to work,
V<sub>GS</sub> must exceed the threshold when the low side is at ground — that is,
the low-side supply must be comfortably above the 1.5 V worst-case threshold.
With a 1.8 V low side the margin is only 300 mV, and the threshold moves with
temperature, so that is thin. [2]

**Input capacitance is 27 pF** and reverse transfer capacitance 6 pF. [1] Those
small numbers are why the circuit works at I²C speeds; a power MOSFET's hundreds
of picofarads would slow the bus badly.

**Leakage is small but not zero, and it grows sharply when hot.** onsemi
specifies drain leakage at 0.5 µA maximum at 50 V and 25 °C, rising to 5 µA at a
125 °C junction. Gate leakage is up to ±100 nA at ±20 V. [2]

## Watch out for

- **Ohms of on-resistance.** Not a power switch. For that, use the AO3400A.
- **Pull-up resistors are part of the circuit**, one on each side, and their
  value sets the bus speed. Too large and the edges are slow; too small and the
  MOSFET cannot pull low.
- **It does not work for push-pull signals** such as SPI — the level-shifter
  circuit needs open-drain lines with pull-ups.
- **300 mW dissipation** in SOT-323 is the limit.
