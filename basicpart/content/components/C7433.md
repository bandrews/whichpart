# OP07CDR

## What it is

The OP07 is the classic precision op-amp: a single amplifier whose whole purpose
is to have almost no input offset voltage and almost no drift. Where a general-
purpose part like the LM358 might be several millivolts off, the OP07C is
typically 60 µV off, and drifts by only about 0.5 µV per degree. [1]

That accuracy is what you want for amplifying a thermocouple, a strain gauge, or
a shunt-resistor current sense — anywhere a small DC signal must be measured
faithfully. What you give up is speed: it is a 0.6 MHz part with a 0.3 V/µs slew
rate, so it is no use for audio or fast signals. [1]

## What the datasheet actually says

**Input offset drift is specified, and it is excellent.** ±0.5 µV/°C typical for
the OP07C, against a ±2.5 µV/°C maximum for the OP07D — the same
typical-versus-guaranteed split as the offset voltage itself. Over a 40 °C swing
±0.5 µV/°C is 20 µV of additional error, still less than most op-amps' offset at
room temperature. [1]

**Common-mode rejection is 120 dB typical, 97 dB minimum** over 0 °C to 70 °C at
±13 V common-mode. High CMRR is what lets you measure a small differential signal
riding on a large common-mode voltage. [1]

**No external components required.** The datasheet says this in its feature list
— the part is internally compensated and internally trimmed, so the classic
offset-null potentiometer is optional rather than expected. [1]

**TI names successors.** The datasheet points to the OPA207 (lower power) and
OPA202 (better capacitive load drive) for new designs needing wider temperature
range or better performance. The OP07 remains in production; TI is simply
signposting. [1]

## Watch out for

- **0 °C to +70 °C.** Precision and commercial temperature grade in the same
  part. If you need precision *and* −40 °C, look elsewhere.
- **It needs a dual supply, or at least 6 V.** Many people reach for the OP07
  in a 5 V single-supply design and find it does not work; that is below the
  recommended minimum.
- **Fast shorts to the positive supply can destroy it.** The datasheet permits a
  continuous short to ground or to the negative supply, but explicitly warns
  against fast ramping shorts to V+. [1]
- **0.6 MHz is genuinely slow.** Precision and speed are separate axes; this part
  buys one with the other.
