# AO3401A

## What it is

A P-channel MOSFET in SOT-23 — the mirror image of the AO3400A. Where an
N-channel part switches the ground side of a load, a P-channel part switches the
positive side, which is what you want when the load must have a solid ground, or
when you are building reverse-polarity protection. [1]

Like its N-channel sibling it works from low gate voltages: Alpha & Omega
specifies operation down to a 2.5 V gate drive. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Channel | P-channel, enhancement mode, trench technology [1] | High-side switching. Note that all its voltages are negative with respect to the source. |
| Drain-source voltage | −30 V [1] | The same 30 V magnitude as the AO3400A. |
| Continuous drain current | −4.0 A at V<sub>GS</sub> = −10 V [1] | Lower than the N-channel part's 5.7 A — P-channel silicon is inherently worse, and this is normal. |
| On-resistance at stated drive | Under 50 mΩ at V<sub>GS</sub> = −10 V, under 60 mΩ at −4.5 V, under 85 mΩ at −2.5 V [1] | Roughly double the AO3400A's at every drive level. That is the price of high-side switching. |
| Gate threshold | −0.5 V minimum, −0.9 V typical, −1.3 V maximum at V<sub>DS</sub> = V<sub>GS</sub>, I<sub>D</sub> = −250 mA [1] | Again, threshold is where conduction begins, not where it is fully on. |
| Operating temperature | The datasheet's thermal figures assume T<sub>J(MAX)</sub> = 150 °C; the catalog does not record an operating temperature for this part [1] [2] | See `ISSUES.md` — the catalog's temperature field is empty. |

## What the datasheet actually says

**To turn it off, the gate must be at the source voltage, not at ground.** This is
the thing that trips people up with P-channel high-side switches. If the source is
at 12 V and you drive the gate to 0 V with a microcontroller pin, the part is
fully on — and you cannot turn it off with a 3.3 V pin, because 3.3 V is still
8.7 V below the source. The standard arrangement is a small N-channel MOSFET or
transistor pulling the P-channel gate down, with a resistor pulling it up to the
source.

**Gate-source voltage is also a limit.** With a 12 V source and the gate at
ground, V<sub>GS</sub> is −12 V — check that against the absolute maximum before
assuming it is safe.

**On-resistance rises with temperature**, from 41 mΩ typical to 62 mΩ typical at
T<sub>J</sub> = 125 °C at −10 V drive. [1]

**As a reverse-polarity protector it is wired backwards on purpose** — drain to
the supply, source to the load — so the body diode conducts in the correct
polarity and the channel then shorts it out with far less drop than a diode.

## Watch out for

- **Turning it off needs gate drive at the source potential.** Almost always a
  second small transistor.
- **Check V<sub>GS</sub> against the absolute maximum** on higher-voltage rails.
- **85 mΩ at 2.5 V drive** is nearly double the AO3400A's — factor the extra loss
  into your thermal budget.
- **The catalog has no operating-temperature entry** for this part. See
  `ISSUES.md`.
