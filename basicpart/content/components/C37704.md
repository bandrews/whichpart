# BAT54C,215

## What it is

A Schottky diode drops less voltage when conducting than an ordinary silicon
diode and switches faster. The BAT54C puts two of them in a SOT-23 with their
cathodes joined, which is the arrangement you want for combining two power
sources, or for clamping a signal to a rail. [1]

The `C` suffix is the configuration. BAT54 is a single diode, BAT54A is
common-anode, BAT54S is series, BAT54C is common-cathode. They all share a
package and a pinout, so getting the letter wrong is an easy and annoying
mistake. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Function | Dual planar Schottky barrier diode, common cathode, with an integrated guard ring for stress protection [1] | The guard ring is what makes it more rugged than a bare Schottky junction. |
| Repetitive peak reverse voltage | 30 V [1] | Modest. This is a signal and small-power part, not a rectifier. |
| Average forward current | 200 mA at 25 °C; 300 mA repetitive peak; total power dissipation 250 mW [1] | 250 mW is the real constraint in a SOT-23. |
| Forward voltage | 800 mV maximum at 100 mA (pulsed, 25 °C) [1] | Lower than a silicon diode's ~1 V, but not the 300 mV a power Schottky manages — small Schottkys trade forward drop for low leakage. |
| Recovery or capacitance | Diode capacitance 10 pF maximum at 1 V reverse and 1 MHz; reverse recovery time 5 ns maximum. Reverse current 2 µA maximum at V<sub>R</sub> = 25 V [1] | 10 pF and 5 ns are what "ultra high-speed switching" means in practice — fast enough for signal steering and clamping. The 2 µA of leakage is high compared with a silicon signal diode; that is the Schottky trade-off. |
| Operating temperature | Junction temperature up to 150 °C [1] | Junction, not ambient. |

## What the datasheet actually says

**Nexperia lists four applications**, and they map to four common circuits:
ultra-high-speed switching, line termination, voltage clamping, and reverse
polarity protection. The common-cathode pair is particularly suited to clamping a
signal between two rails, or to OR-ing two supplies onto one node. [1]

**The forward-voltage figure is a pulsed measurement.** 800 mV maximum at 100 mA
is specified with a pulse width of 300 µs or less at a duty cycle of 0.02 —
that is, self-heating is deliberately excluded. Under continuous DC the drop will
be a little higher. [1]

**Reverse current is 2 µA at 25 V.** Schottky diodes leak far more than PN
junction diodes, and leakage rises steeply with temperature. If you are using
this to isolate a battery, that leakage is a continuous drain. [1]

## Watch out for

- **Check the suffix letter.** C, A, S and plain BAT54 look identical and are
  wired differently.
- **250 mW total dissipation** for both diodes together, not each.
- **Schottky leakage is not negligible** in battery or high-impedance circuits,
  and it worsens with temperature.
- **30 V only.** For rectifying a 24 V supply, use a higher-voltage part such as
  the SS14.
