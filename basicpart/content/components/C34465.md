# LM2576SX-5.0/NOPB

## What it is

The LM2576 is the earlier member of TI's SIMPLE SWITCHER family: a fixed 5 V,
3 A buck regulator that runs from inputs up to 40 V and needs only four external
components. Functionally it does the same job as the LM2596. [1]

The difference is switching frequency: 52 kHz here against 150 kHz for the
LM2596. That means a substantially larger inductor and output capacitor for the
same ripple. In this catalog it is also more than twice the price and has far
less stock, so the LM2596 is the better default unless you specifically need
LM2576 compatibility with an existing design. [1] [2]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Output voltage | Fixed 5 V (this order code); the family also offers 3.3 V, 12 V, 15 V and adjustable versions [1] | Fixed output, no divider to get wrong. |
| Output current | Specified 3 A [1] | Same headline current as the LM2596. |
| Input voltage range | Up to 40 V (up to 60 V for the HV version) [1]; the catalog records 4 V to 40 V for this fixed-5 V part [2] | The HV variant is a genuinely different part number, not an option on this one. |
| Switching frequency | 52 kHz [2]; TI specifies a fixed-frequency internal oscillator for this family [1] | The lowest switching frequency of any converter in this catalog, and the reason it needs the biggest inductor. |
| Efficiency | Better than a linear regulator across the range; TI names synchronous successors for higher efficiency [1] | Non-synchronous with an external diode, so losses are higher than a modern synchronous part. |
| Operating temperature | −40 °C to +125 °C junction [2] | A junction limit, dependent on the copper attached to the TO-263 tab. |

## What the datasheet actually says

**TI names three replacements in the first feature bullet:** the LMR51430
(4.5–36 V, 3 A synchronous), the LM76003 (3.5–60 V, 3.5 A synchronous), and the
TLVM13630 power module. That is a stronger signposting than most parts get. The
LM2576 remains in production. [1]

**Output tolerance is ±4 % maximum over line and load conditions** for the
adjustable version, whose range is 1.23 V to 37 V (57 V on the HV part). [1]

**52 kHz has one advantage:** at low switching frequencies, switching losses are
small and the design is very forgiving of layout. That is the whole reason this
family was popular — it is hard to make it misbehave.

## Watch out for

- **The inductor will be physically large.** At 52 kHz you need something in the
  hundreds of microhenries for reasonable ripple, and it must carry over 3 A.
- **More than twice the price of the LM2596 here**, for the same output. Choose
  it only if you need the exact part.
- **Stock is thin** — 3,192 units at the snapshot date.
- **Non-synchronous.** External catch diode required, and it dominates the
  losses at high current.
