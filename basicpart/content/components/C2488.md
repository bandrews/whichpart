> **Note on sources.** The part supplied here is `MDD (Microdiode
> Semiconductor)`-branded, and MDD's own datasheet is served only through LCSC,
> which blocks automated retrieval. MB10S is an industry-standard type, so this
> note cites the JLCPCB/LCSC catalog record as `[1]` for the part actually being

> flagging where the two differ, which here they do in an important way. See

## What it is

A bridge rectifier is four diodes arranged so that whichever way the input
polarity goes, the output comes out the same way round. The MB10S packs all four
into a small surface-mount body with four pins: two for the AC or unknown-polarity
input, two for the DC output. [1]

Two everyday uses: rectifying a low-voltage AC transformer into DC, and making a
DC input polarity-proof so a user cannot destroy the board by plugging the barrel
jack in backwards. [1]

## What the datasheet actually says

**Two diode drops, not one.** This is the number that surprises people. At any
moment the current passes through one diode on the way in and another on the way
out, so the output is roughly 2.2 V below the peak input. Rectifying a 5 V AC
supply leaves you about 4.8 V peak, and after ripple, less. For low-voltage work
that loss is severe. [1]

**The current rating depends on what you solder it to, and the catalog's figure
is optimistic.** EIC specifies 0.5 A on a glass-epoxy PCB with 13 × 13 mm pads,
and 0.8 A only on an aluminium substrate — two different numbers for the same
part, distinguished purely by how the heat gets out. The thermal resistance is
85 °C/W even with those generous pads. The catalog's 1 A exceeds both. Treat
0.5 A as the design figure on a normal FR4 board. [1][2]

**Heat is the constraint.** Half an amp through two diodes at about 1 V each is
1 W in a small surface-mount package. At 85 °C/W that is an 85 °C rise — which is
why the rating is what it is, and why giving the pads real copper matters.

**Reverse leakage is 5 µA at 1,000 V and 25 °C, rising to 100 µA at 125 °C** —
typical behaviour for silicon rectifiers, and a twentyfold increase worth knowing
about in a warm enclosure. [1][2]

## Watch out for

- **You lose two diode drops, about 2.2 V.** Budget for it, and consider an
  active bridge or a P-channel MOSFET for reverse protection in low-voltage
  designs.
- **Design to 0.5 A, not the catalog's 1 A**, unless you have MDD's own
  datasheet in front of you. [1][2]
- **It gets hot.** Compute 2 × V<sub>F</sub> × I and give the package copper —
  the published ratings assume 13 × 13 mm pads. [2]
- **Not for switching converters.** These are 50/60 Hz rectifier diodes.
- **Mains-adjacent circuits need proper clearance** on the board.
