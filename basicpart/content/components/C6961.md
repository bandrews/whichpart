# TL072CDT

## What it is

The TL072 is a dual op-amp with JFET inputs. That input stage is the whole story:
it draws picoamps rather than nanoamps, so it can amplify signals from very
high-impedance sources — piezo pickups, electret microphones, photodiodes,
guitar pickups — without loading them down. [1]

It is also fast for its age and price: 4 MHz gain-bandwidth and a 16 V/µs slew
rate, against the NE5532's 12 MHz and 5 V/µs and the LM358's 1 MHz. Combined with
0.01 % distortion, that is why it is a staple of audio circuits and effects
pedals. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Function | Dual JFET-input operational amplifier, internally compensated, with output short-circuit protection and latch-up-free operation [1] | Two amplifiers per package; no external compensation. |
| Supply voltage | ±18 V absolute maximum; the catalog records a 6 V to 36 V single-supply / ±3 V to ±18 V operating range [1] [2] | A split-supply part in practice, though it works single-supply if the signal is biased to mid-rail. |
| Input offset voltage | 3 mV typical, 10 mV maximum for the plain TL072 grade (the TL072A is 6 mV maximum, the TL072B 3 mV) [1] | Loose. This is an AC part; do not use it where DC accuracy matters without correction. |
| Gain bandwidth | 4 MHz typical, 2.5 MHz minimum [1] | Comfortably wide for audio, with headroom for feedback. |
| Output swing | ±12 V typical into 2 kΩ, ±13.5 V typical into 10 kΩ, on ±15 V supplies; common-mode input range ±11 V [1] | About 3 V short of each rail into 2 kΩ. Not rail-to-rail. |
| Supply current | 1.4 mA typical, 2.5 mA maximum for both amplifiers, no load [1] | Modest — noticeably lower than the NE5532's 6 mA. |
| Operating temperature | 0 °C to +70 °C for the `C` grade, which is this part. (The `I` grade covers −40 °C to +125 °C) [1] | **Commercial grade.** The suffix letter is the temperature range. |

## What the datasheet actually says

**Input offset current is 5 pA typical at 25 °C — but 4 nA typical over the full
temperature range.** JFET input current roughly doubles every 10 °C, so the
picoamp figure is a room-temperature number. At 70 °C it is nearly a thousand
times worse. If your circuit depends on ultra-low input current, check it at your
actual operating temperature. [1]

**Input resistance is 10¹² Ω.** That is a teraohm — effectively an open circuit
next to any real source impedance, which is exactly what a piezo or photodiode
wants. [1]

**Total harmonic distortion is 0.01 % typical** at 1 kHz, 2 V peak-to-peak, into
2 kΩ with 20 dB of gain. Low enough that the rest of the circuit will dominate. [1]

**Equivalent input noise is 15 nV/√Hz at 1 kHz** with a 100 Ω source. That is
three times the NE5532's 5 nV/√Hz, so for low-impedance sources like a moving-coil
cartridge the NE5532 is quieter; for high-impedance sources the TL072 wins
because it is not loading the source. [1]

## Watch out for

- **0 °C to +70 °C** for this `C` grade. Order the TL072I if you need
  industrial temperatures.
- **Low input current only at room temperature.** Rechecking at your maximum
  operating temperature is worth doing.
- **JFET inputs are noisier than bipolar** at low source impedance. Choose the
  part to match the source, not by reputation.
- **The input voltage must never exceed the supply magnitude or 15 V, whichever
  is less** — the datasheet states this explicitly.
