# US1M

> **Note on sources.** The part supplied here is `MDD (Microdiode
> Semiconductor)`-branded, and MDD's own datasheet is served only through LCSC,
> which blocks automated retrieval. US1M is an industry-standard type, so this
> note cites the JLCPCB/LCSC catalog record as `[1]` for the part actually being
> supplied, and Nexperia's US1M datasheet as `[2]` for the type — flagging where
> the two differ. See `ISSUES.md`.

## What it is

Where the SS14 is a Schottky rated for 40 V, the US1M is a silicon ultrafast
recovery diode rated for 1,000 V — twenty-five times the voltage. It gives up the
Schottky's low forward drop in exchange for standing off mains-level voltages and
still switching off in 75 ns. [1][2]

Use it in offline (mains-connected) supplies, snubber networks, flyback
converters, and anywhere a rectifier has to handle high voltage and switch
quickly. It also appears as a faster replacement for the slow 1N4007 in circuits
where recovery time matters. [2]

## What the datasheet actually says

**You cannot run 1 A through this continuously on a bare footprint.** Nexperia
rates total power dissipation at 0.63 W on a standard footprint, rising to 0.93 W
with a 1 cm² copper pad on the cathode, both at 25 °C ambient — with thermal
resistances of 200 K/W and 135 K/W respectively. At 1.65 V of drop, 1 A means
1.65 W, well past either limit. The 1 A figure is an *average* current at 50 %
duty with the solder point actively held cool. Give the cathode real copper and
work out your actual dissipation. [2]

**Reverse leakage rises fiftyfold when hot.** At 25 °C the catalog's 5 µA at
1,000 V is right, and it is beautifully low. At 125 °C the same part leaks up to
250 µA. In a high-voltage circuit that is 0.25 W of loss doing nothing useful,
and it climbs with temperature — worth checking in a design that runs warm. [2]

**Fast recovery is what you are paying for.** During recovery the diode conducts
backwards briefly; in a switching converter that reverse current flows through
the switch and turns into heat and electromagnetic interference. 75 ns keeps that
manageable at typical converter frequencies; the microseconds a 1N4007 takes
would not. [2]

## Watch out for

- **Check the thermal path before assuming 1 A.** 0.63 W on a standard footprint
  is the real constraint. [2]
- **A Schottky is better below about 40 V.** Use the SS14 (C7420316) there; the
  US1M's voltage rating is wasted and its drop is three times higher.
- **Leakage at temperature is 250 µA, not 5 µA.** [2]
- **Not fast enough for high-frequency resonant work.** 75 ns is fast for a
  1,000 V part, but a silicon-carbide diode is faster still.
- **Mains-adjacent circuits need clearance and creepage** on the board, whatever
  the diode is rated for.
- **The branded part may not match Nexperia's numbers exactly.** Where a figure
  matters, get MDD's own datasheet from your supplier. [1]
