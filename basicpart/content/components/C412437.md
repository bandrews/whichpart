## What it is

Where the SS14 is a Schottky rated for 40 V, the US1M is a silicon fast-recovery
diode rated for 1,000 V — twenty-five times the voltage. It gives up the
Schottky's low forward drop in exchange for standing off mains-level voltages and
still switching off in 75 ns. [1]

Use it in offline (mains-connected) supplies, snubber networks, flyback
converters, and anywhere a rectifier has to handle high voltage and switch
quickly. It also appears as a general-purpose replacement for the slow 1N4007 in
circuits where recovery time matters. [1]

## What the datasheet actually says

The catalog record gives reverse leakage of **5 µA at 1,000 V** — very low, and
the opposite of a Schottky's behaviour. This is why silicon diodes remain the
choice for high-voltage blocking. [1]

The 75 ns recovery figure is what separates a "fast recovery" diode from a
general-purpose rectifier. During recovery the diode conducts backwards briefly;
in a switching converter that reverse current flows through the switch and turns
into heat and EMI. 75 ns keeps that manageable at typical converter frequencies;
microseconds would not. [1]

## Watch out for

- **1.65 V of drop at 1 A is 1.65 W.** In an SMA package that needs copper. Check
  the thermal path before assuming continuous 1 A.
- **A Schottky is better below about 40 V.** Use the SS14 there; the US1M's
  voltage rating is wasted and its drop is three times higher.
- **Not fast enough for high-frequency resonant work.** 75 ns is fast for a
  1,000 V part, but a modern SiC diode is faster still.
- **Mains-adjacent circuits need clearance and creepage** on the board, whatever
  the diode is rated for.
