# HT7533-1

## What it is

A three-terminal 3.3 V regulator built in CMOS rather than bipolar. That choice
is what makes it a *genuine* low-dropout part: it loses about 100 mV at light
load instead of the 2 V a classic 78xx needs, and it draws 2.5 µA of quiescent
current instead of milliamps. [1]

Use it where a battery has to last: a sensor node, a remote logger, anything that
spends most of its life asleep. The 24 V input rating also makes it usable
directly from a 12 V or 24 V supply. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Output voltage | Fixed 3.3 V, ±3 % (3.201 V to 3.399 V at 10 mA) [1] | Two significant figures of accuracy is fine for digital loads, marginal for an ADC reference. |
| Output current | 60 mA minimum, 100 mA typical [1] | The "100mA" in the datasheet title is the *typical* figure; the guaranteed minimum is 60 mA. Design to 60 mA. |
| Input voltage range | Up to 24 V; absolute maximum 26 V [1] | Wide enough to regulate straight from a 12 V or 24 V rail. |
| Dropout voltage | 100 mV typical at 1 mA output [1] | At higher currents the drop rises — this is a light-load figure. |
| Quiescent current | 2.5 µA typical, 5 µA maximum, no load [1] | This is the number that matters for battery life, and it is 2,400× better than the L78M05's 6 mA. |
| Output accuracy | ±3 % tolerance; load regulation 60 mV typical from 1 mA to 50 mA; line regulation 0.2 %/V; temperature coefficient 0.5 mV/°C [1] | Load regulation of 60 mV is about 1.8 % — visible if the load current swings a lot. |
| Operating temperature | −40 °C to +85 °C [1] | Industrial range. |

## What the datasheet actually says

**Power dissipation is the real limit, and it differs by package.** Holtek gives
250 mW for the SOT-89 and TO-92 packages but only 150 mW for SOT-25. This part is
SOT-89. Regulating 12 V down to 3.3 V at 25 mA is 218 mW — already close to the
ceiling. The 100 mA figure is only reachable at small input-output differences. [1]

**Temperature coefficient is specified over 0 °C to 70 °C**, a narrower window
than the −40 °C to +85 °C operating range. Accuracy outside that window is not
characterised. [1]

**These are fixed regulators that can be used with external components to obtain
variable voltages**, Holtek notes — the usual divider trick, at the cost of the
quiescent current advantage. [1]

## Watch out for

- **The catalog says 30 V input; the datasheet we could obtain says 24 V**, with
  26 V as the absolute maximum. See `ISSUES.md`. Design to 24 V.
- **Dropout is a light-load number.** 100 mV at 1 mA does not mean 100 mV at
  100 mA.
- **Package dissipation, not the current rating, is the constraint.** Compute
  (V<sub>in</sub> − 3.3 V) × I<sub>out</sub> against 250 mW.
- **CMOS LDOs are fussy about output capacitors.** Follow the datasheet's
  application circuit rather than substituting whatever is on the reel.
