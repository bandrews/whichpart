# CP2102-GMR

## What it is

The CP2102 is a USB-to-serial bridge: plug it into a computer and it appears as a
COM port, while on the other side it speaks ordinary asynchronous serial to your
microcontroller. It is how most development boards let you print debug messages
to a terminal. [1]

What distinguishes it from cheaper alternatives is how little it needs around it.
Silicon Labs integrates the USB transceiver (no external series resistors), the
clock (no crystal), the voltage regulator, and the power-on reset. In practice
the whole circuit is the chip, a USB connector, and a couple of capacitors. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Function | Single-chip USB-to-UART bridge with integrated transceiver, clock, regulator and power-on reset [1] | Fewer external parts than any competing bridge, and no crystal to source or lay out. |
| Signalling standard | USB Specification 2.0 compliant, full-speed (12 Mbps); asynchronous serial with all handshaking and modem interface signals [1] | Full modem control (RTS, CTS, DTR, DSR, DCD, RI) — many cheaper bridges give you only TX and RX. |
| Maximum data rate | 300 bps to 1 Mbps on the UART side [1] | Comfortably faster than the 115,200 baud most people use. |
| Supply voltage | Self-powered 3.0 V to 3.6 V, or USB bus-powered 4.0 V to 5.25 V; absolute maximum 4.2 V on V<sub>DD</sub> [1] | Bus-powered is the usual mode: the on-chip regulator makes 3.3 V from USB's 5 V and can supply your board. |
| Isolation or protection | None. Maximum output current sunk by RST or any I/O pin is 100 mA; maximum total current through V<sub>DD</sub> and GND is 500 mA [1] | There is no galvanic isolation — if you need that, add a digital isolator such as the ADuM1201. |
| Operating temperature | −40 °C to +85 °C [1] | Industrial range. |

## What the datasheet actually says

**The USB descriptors live in on-chip EEPROM.** 1,024 bytes hold the vendor ID,
product ID, serial number, power descriptor, release number and product
description strings — and on the CP2102 that memory is rewritable. (The CP2109
variant uses one-time-programmable EPROM instead.) A unique serial number per
board means the operating system can assign a stable COM port. [1]

**Buffers are 576 bytes receive and 640 bytes transmit.** That is what absorbs a
burst while the host is busy. [1]

**Hardware or X-On/X-Off handshaking is supported**, along with event characters
and line-break transmission. [1]

**Drivers are the reason to choose it.** Silicon Labs supplies royalty-free
virtual COM port drivers for Windows, macOS and Linux, and modern Linux and macOS
have them built in. This is the practical difference between the CP2102 and
cheaper bridges whose drivers can be a nuisance. [1]

## Watch out for

- **It costs $2.19 in ones** — significantly more than the CH340. You are paying
  for the integration, the driver support and the on-chip EEPROM.
- **No isolation.** USB ground is tied to your board's ground.
- **The exposed pad on the QFN-28 must be soldered.** It is the ground
  connection, not just a thermal pad.
- **Bus-powered mode limits what you can draw.** USB will give you 500 mA at
  most, and only after enumeration.
