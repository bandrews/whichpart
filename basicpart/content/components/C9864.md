# TPS5430DDAR

## What it is

The TPS5430 is a step-down (buck) switching regulator with the switching
transistor built in. Give it an input between 5.5 V and 36 V and a pair of
feedback resistors, and it produces a regulated output at up to 3 A continuously.
Because it switches rather than burns off the difference as heat, it is far more
efficient than a linear regulator when the input-to-output gap is large. [1]

The design goal of this part is low external parts count: the loop compensation
is internal, so you need an inductor, a catch diode, input and output capacitors,
and two resistors. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Output voltage | Adjustable, down to 1.22 V, with 1.5 % initial accuracy [1] | Two resistors set it. The 1.22 V floor is the internal reference. |
| Output current | Up to 3 A continuous, 4 A peak [1] | Enough to run a small single-board computer or a bank of LEDs. |
| Input voltage range | 5.5 V to 36 V [1] | Covers 12 V and 24 V industrial rails with margin, and survives a 12 V automotive system's normal excursions. |
| Switching frequency | Fixed 500 kHz [1] | Fast enough to keep the inductor and output capacitor small; not so fast that layout becomes critical. |
| Efficiency | Up to 95 %, enabled by a 100 mΩ integrated MOSFET switch [1] | The headline number is at a favourable operating point; expect less at low output voltage or light load. |
| Operating temperature | −40 °C to +125 °C junction [2] | A junction limit, so it depends on how well you sink heat from the exposed pad. |

## What the datasheet actually says

**It is not synchronous.** The chip contains the high-side switch but you must
supply an external catch diode. A Schottky diode is the usual choice, and its
forward drop is where a noticeable share of the losses go at high current. [1] [2]

**Input feedforward improves line regulation.** TI calls this out as a feature: the
control loop compensates for input-voltage changes directly, so the output moves
less when the input does. [1]

**Compensation is internal.** This is the trade-off that makes the part easy to
use — and the reason you have less freedom to tune the loop for an unusual
output filter than you would with an externally compensated part such as the
TPS54331. [1]

**Watch the BOOT-to-PH capacitor rating.** A datasheet footnote warns that some
devices develop more than 6 V (but less than 10 V) between BOOT and PH during
normal operation, and that the bootstrap capacitor must therefore be rated for at
least 10 V. This is easy to get wrong when substituting a part from a
generic library. [1]

## Watch out for

- **The exposed pad is a thermal connection, not optional.** At 3 A the package
  needs copper under it to stay within its junction limit.
- **You must add the catch diode.** Omitting it, or choosing one with too high a
  forward voltage, costs efficiency and can destroy the part.
- **Switching layout matters.** Keep the input capacitor, the switch node, and
  the diode in a tight loop, and keep the feedback trace away from the switch
  node.
- **Minimum input is 5.5 V.** It cannot step 5 V down to 3.3 V.
