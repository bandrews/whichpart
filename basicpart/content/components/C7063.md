# SN75176BDR

## What it is

RS-485 is the industrial workhorse for sending serial data hundreds of metres
through electrically noisy places, and the SN75176B is the transceiver that made
it commonplace. It contains one differential driver and one differential
receiver, each separately enabled, sharing the same pair of bus pins so you can
run half-duplex on two wires. [1]

It is a 5 V part, and an old one, but it remains a sensible choice when your
system is already 5 V and you want something thoroughly proven. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Function | One differential driver and one differential receiver with individual enables, three-state outputs [1] | Half-duplex on two wires, or full duplex on four if you use two devices. |
| Signalling standard | Meets or exceeds ANSI TIA/EIA-422-B and TIA/EIA-485-A, and ITU V.11 and X.27 [1] | Interoperates with any compliant RS-485 or RS-422 device. |
| Maximum data rate | 10 Mbit/s per the catalog record [2]; the datasheet specifies driver and receiver propagation delays rather than a headline signalling rate [1] | Real-world rate is set by cable length as much as by the chip. |
| Supply voltage | 4.75 V to 5.25 V (absolute maximum 7 V) [1] | A 5 V-only part. For 3.3 V systems, use a 3.3 V transceiver instead. |
| Isolation or protection | Bus terminals tolerate −7 V to +12 V (separately or common mode); thermal shutdown; driver positive and negative current limiting; receiver input impedance 12 kΩ minimum; ±200 mV receiver sensitivity with 50 mV typical hysteresis [1] | The −7 V to +12 V range is what lets nodes with different ground potentials talk to each other — the whole point of RS-485. |
| Operating temperature | 0 °C to +70 °C for the SN75176B [1] | **Commercial grade.** The SN65176B covers −40 °C to +105 °C in the same package. |

## What the datasheet actually says

**±200 mV receiver sensitivity with 50 mV of hysteresis** is what gives RS-485 its
noise immunity: the receiver only needs a fifth of a volt of difference to decide,
and the hysteresis stops it chattering on a marginal signal. [1]

**The driver can source or sink ±60 mA maximum.** That is a lot of drive, and it
is why an RS-485 bus can carry many loads over long cable. [1]

**Supply current is 42 mA typical, 70 mA maximum for the whole package with
outputs enabled and no load.** This is not a low-power device by modern
standards. [1]

**There is no fail-safe biasing built in.** Unlike some later transceivers, this
part does not guarantee a known receiver output when the bus is idle and
undriven. External bias resistors are the usual fix.

## Watch out for

- **0 °C to +70 °C.** Industrial RS-485 links often run in unheated spaces; the
  SN65176B is the part to order if so.
- **Termination and biasing are external.** 120 Ω at each end of the bus, plus
  bias resistors if you need a defined idle state.
- **5 V only.** A 3.3 V microcontroller needs level shifting on the logic side,
  or a 3.3 V transceiver such as the SP3485EN.
- **70 mA is real current.** Budget it, especially in bus-powered designs.
