# TPS54331DR

## What it is

The TPS54331 is a 3 A step-down switching regulator in a plain SOIC-8. It covers
a 3.5 V to 28 V input range and can regulate down to 0.8 V, which makes it
suitable for generating core rails as well as the usual 5 V and 3.3 V. [1]

Compared with the TPS5430 it starts from a lower input voltage, regulates to a
lower output, has a much lower quiescent current at light load, and costs about
half as much. What you give up is the internal compensation — this part is
externally compensated, so it needs a few more components and a little more
design thought. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Output voltage | Adjustable down to 0.8 V; the internal reference is 0.8 V with guaranteed limits of 0.772 V and 0.828 V, so ±3.5 % before your divider's own tolerance [1] | The lower reference means it can generate 1.2 V or 1.8 V rails that the TPS5430 cannot reach. Note the reference is not a precision one — do not expect ±1 % rails from it. |
| Output current | Up to 3 A continuous, via an integrated high-side MOSFET of 80 mΩ typical and 150 mΩ maximum at 12 V in, rising to 115 mΩ typical and 200 mΩ maximum at 3.5 V in [1] | Lower switch resistance than the TPS5430's 100 mΩ, so slightly better efficiency at full load — but the advantage shrinks at low input voltage, where the switch is measurably worse. |
| Input voltage range | 3.5 V to 28 V [1] | Starting at 3.5 V means it can run from a single lithium cell or a 5 V rail. |
| Switching frequency | Fixed 570 kHz typical, guaranteed between 456 kHz and 684 kHz [1] | Slightly faster than the TPS5430, allowing marginally smaller passives. Size the inductor at the 456 kHz end, where ripple current is largest. |
| Efficiency | Pulse-skipping Eco-mode maintains high efficiency at light loads; shutdown quiescent current typically 1 µA [1] | Eco-mode is the reason this part suits battery designs — a fixed-frequency converter wastes power at no load. |
| Operating temperature | −40 °C to +150 °C junction [1] | A generous junction ceiling, though thermal design still governs what you can actually do. |

## What the datasheet actually says

**Eco-mode is pulse skipping, not a separate mode you enable.** It engages by
itself once the peak switch current falls to about 160 mA. Below that the
converter stops switching every cycle, which cuts switching losses but also makes
the output ripple lower in frequency and less regular. If you are powering
something ripple-sensitive at very light load, that is worth measuring. [1]

**The undervoltage lockout threshold is programmable.** You can set the input
voltage at which the converter starts, which matters when running from a source
with high impedance — it stops the converter from repeatedly trying to start and
collapsing the input. [1]

**Slow-start is adjustable, and TI recommends you actually set it.** A capacitor
on the SS pin, charged by an internal 2 µA source, sets the ramp and limits inrush
current at power-up. There is no internal slow-start at all, so leaving the pin
open means the output ramps as fast as the current limit allows — TI calls
programming it "highly recommended". [1]

**Protection is comprehensive:** cycle-by-cycle current limit, frequency
foldback, overvoltage transient protection, and thermal shutdown. [1]

## Watch out for

- **External compensation means extra parts.** You need to size the compensation
  network for your output filter; TI's application section walks through it, but
  it is not a copy-and-paste part in the way the TPS5430 is.
- **Non-synchronous.** You still add an external catch diode.
- **Plain SOIC-8 without an exposed pad** in this order code — thermal
  performance depends on the copper attached to the pins, so check the
  dissipation at your operating point.
- **Switching layout still matters.** Tight input loop, short switch node,
  feedback routed away from it.

## In this catalog

Preferred Extended part in SOIC-8. At the 2026-07-24 snapshot: 84,270 in stock,
$0.44 at quantity 1, falling to $0.27 at 5,000 — noticeably cheaper than the
TPS5430 for the same output current. The catalog attributes record 3.5 V–28 V
input, 800 mV–25 V adjustable output, 3 A, 570 kHz, 110 µA quiescent current,
external compensation and −40 °C to +150 °C junction temperature, all consistent
with the datasheet. The 110 µA is the non-switching operating supply current
(190 µA maximum); in shutdown the part draws about 1 µA. [1] [2]
