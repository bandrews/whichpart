# TP4057-42-SOT26-R

> **Note on sources.** No manufacturer datasheet for the TP4057 could be
> retrieved: the LCSC-hosted copy is blocked to automated fetching and Nanjing
> Top Power's own site did not respond. Every figure below therefore comes from
> the JLCPCB/LCSC catalog record and is cited as `[1]`. Where the TP4056's
> datasheet is quoted for family context it is cited separately as `[2]`. See
> `ISSUES.md`.

## What it is

The TP4057 is the smaller member of the same family as the TP4056: a standalone
constant-current / constant-voltage charger for a single lithium cell, but in a
six-pin SOT-23 rather than an 8-pin SOIC, and rated for 500 mA instead of 1 A. [1]

Choose it when board space matters more than charge speed — a wearable, a small
sensor, anything with a modest cell where 500 mA is already a reasonable charge
rate. [1]

## Key specifications

| Specification | Value | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Function | Standalone constant-current / constant-voltage single-cell lithium charger [1] | Same job as the TP4056, smaller and lower current. |
| Charge voltage | 4.2 V [1] | The standard lithium-ion float voltage, fixed. |
| Charge current | Up to 500 mA, programmable [1] | Half the TP4056. For a 500 mAh cell that is a 1C charge — already at the upper end of sensible. |
| Input voltage range | 4 V to 9 V [1] | Slightly wider than the TP4056's 4 V–8 V, which gives a little more headroom above USB. |
| Termination and status | Constant-current, constant-voltage, trickle charge, automatic recharge, charge termination, programmable charge current, soft-start and undervoltage lockout [1] | The same feature set as the TP4056. |
| Operating temperature | −40 °C to +85 °C [1] | Industrial range. |

## What the specification implies

**No battery temperature detection.** The catalog record marks this explicitly as
"Not supported", where the TP4056 supports it. If your design needs to stop
charging when the cell is too hot or too cold — which any product sold to
consumers arguably should — the TP4056 is the part with the pin for it. [1]

**No zero-volt battery charging.** Also marked "Not supported". A cell that has
been discharged to 0 V may not be recoverable with this charger. [1]

**Quiescent current is 40 µA**, against the TP4056's 2 µA. In a
battery-powered product where the charger sits idle most of the time, that is a
real difference — 40 µA continuously is about 0.35 Ah per year. [1]

**Thermal limits still apply.** Like the TP4056, this is a *linear* charger: the
difference between input and battery voltage times the charge current is
dissipated as heat in the package. At 500 mA from 5 V into a 3.6 V cell that is
0.7 W in a SOT-23-6, which is a lot for that package. Expect it to run hot and
throttle. [2]

## Watch out for

- **No protection circuit.** Like every charger in this family, it does not
  protect the cell from over-discharge or short circuit. Use a protected cell or
  add a protection IC.
- **No temperature sensing and no 0 V charging.** If you need either, use the
  TP4056.
- **SOT-23-6 has little thermal mass.** 500 mA is a rating, not a promise, once
  the package warms up.
- **The specification here rests on the catalog record alone.** See the source
  note above.
