## What it is

The 431 is one of the most-used analogue parts ever made, and the CJ431 is
Changjing's version of it. It behaves like a Zener diode whose breakdown voltage
you choose with two resistors: connect a divider from the cathode to the
reference pin, and the device holds its cathode at whatever voltage makes the
reference pin sit at 2.5 V. [1]

That makes it three things at once — an adjustable voltage reference, an error
amplifier for a power supply feedback loop, and a precise comparator. It is the
part on the secondary side of almost every isolated switching supply, sensing the
output and telling the optocoupler what to do. [1]

| Supply voltage | Not applicable — it is a two-terminal-plus-reference shunt device [1] | There is no supply pin; the cathode current is what powers it. |
| Input offset voltage | Not applicable; the internal reference is 2.5 V with ±0.5 % tolerance [1] | ±0.5 % is 12.5 mV — good enough to set a regulator's output to within a percent. |

| Supply current | Minimum cathode current for regulation 1 mA [1] | **The key design rule.** Below 1 mA through the device it stops regulating. Size the feed resistor so at least 1 mA always flows. |
| Operating temperature | −25 °C to +85 °C; temperature coefficient 50 ppm/°C [1] | Note the −25 °C floor, narrower than most parts here. 50 ppm/°C is 125 µV/°C on the 2.5 V reference. |

## What the specification implies

**Two resistors set the output.** V<sub>out</sub> = 2.5 V × (1 + R1/R2), where R1
is from cathode to reference and R2 from reference to anode. Tie the reference
pin straight to the cathode and you get a plain 2.5 V shunt reference.

**It must always draw at least 1 mA.** If the load takes all the current
available through the feed resistor, the 431 falls out of regulation and the
voltage collapses. Size the feed resistor for the *minimum* input voltage and the
*maximum* load.

**±0.5 % is a good grade.** The original TL431 family comes in several tolerance
grades, and 0.5 % is the tighter end. For comparison, that is better than most
integrated regulators' output accuracy.

**50 ppm/°C is the temperature coefficient**, so over a 50 °C swing you would
expect roughly 0.25 % of drift — about half the initial tolerance. Both matter
equally.

## Watch out for

- **Guarantee the 1 mA minimum cathode current** across your whole input and load
  range. This is the single most common way a 431 circuit fails.
- **It is a shunt, so it wastes power.** The feed resistor dissipates whatever the
  load does not take.
- **−25 °C, not −40 °C.** Narrower than most parts in this catalog.
- **Stability in a feedback loop needs thought.** A 431 driving an optocoupler is
  a real control loop with its own compensation requirements; copying a reference
  design is wiser than improvising.
