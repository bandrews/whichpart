# CJ431

## What it is

The 431 is one of the most-used analogue parts ever made, and the CJ431 is
Changjiang's version of it — the TO-92 members of the family are even marked
"CJ TL431". It behaves like a Zener diode whose breakdown voltage you choose with
two resistors: connect a divider from the cathode down to the reference pin, and
the device sinks whatever current it takes to hold that pin at 2.5 V. The
datasheet calls it a three-terminal adjustable shunt regulator and offers it as a
direct replacement for Zener diodes. [1]

That makes it three things at once — an adjustable voltage reference, an error
amplifier for a power supply's feedback loop, and a precise comparator. It is the
part on the secondary side of almost every isolated switching supply, watching the
output and telling the optocoupler what to do. [1]

## What the datasheet actually says

**The ±0.5 % in the catalog is not what the datasheet guarantees.** Changjiang
publishes two things about accuracy. The Electrical Characteristics table, which
is the promise attached to the part, gives V<sub>ref</sub> as 2.475–2.525 V —
±1 %. Underneath, a short table headed "Classification of V<sub>ref</sub>" lists
two ranks: 0.5 % (2.487–2.513 V) and 1 % (2.475–2.525 V). The Ordering
Information table, however, offers exactly one SOT-23 line — part number
"CJ431", marking "431" — with no suffix that would let you ask for the 0.5 %
rank. So the grade the catalog implies is a real grade in this datasheet, but the
datasheet gives you no way to order it and does not guarantee it for the plain
CJ431. If your design depends on 0.5 %, confirm the grade with the supplier
before committing. [1][2]

**Temperature drift is bigger than the initial tolerance.** The 50 ppm/°C in the
catalog comes from the datasheet's feature list, where it is described as a
typical figure for the equivalent temperature coefficient. The guaranteed number
is elsewhere: the deviation of the reference voltage over the full −25 °C to
+85 °C range is 4.5 mV typical and **17 mV maximum**. On a 2.5 V reference,
17 mV is 0.68 % — more than the ±0.5 % initial tolerance, and it applies on top
of it. [1][2]

**The reference pin draws current, and it lands in your divider.** The datasheet
prints the real equation next to its test circuit:
V<sub>KA</sub> = V<sub>ref</sub> × (1 + R1/R2) + R1 × I<sub>ref</sub>. With the
10 kΩ top resistor the datasheet itself uses, the 4 µA maximum reference current
adds up to 40 mV of error — comparable to the tolerance you were trying to
achieve. Keep the divider resistors low enough that this term stays small. [1]

**100 mA is not a continuous operating point in this package.** The absolute
maximum cathode current is 150 mA and the "trapping current capability" quoted in
the features is 1 to 100 mA, but SOT-23 dissipation is capped at 300 mW. Even at
the minimum 2.5 V cathode voltage, 100 mA is 250 mW — already at the edge. The
SOT-89 and TO-92 versions of the same die are rated for 500 mW and 770 mW. [1]

## Watch out for

- **Guarantee the 1 mA minimum cathode current** across your whole input and load
  range. This is the single most common way a 431 circuit fails. [1]
- **±0.5 % is not guaranteed by this datasheet** for the plain CJ431 — the
  guaranteed window is ±1 %. [1][2]
- **The CJ431 and CD431 have different SOT-23 pinouts.** On the CJ431, pin 1 is
  the reference, pin 2 the cathode and pin 3 the anode; on the CD431, pin 1 is the
  cathode and pin 2 the reference. Both appear on this one datasheet, in the same
  package. Check your footprint against the CJ431 column. [1]
- **It is a shunt, so it wastes power.** The feed resistor dissipates whatever
  the load does not take, and the device itself burns the rest.
- **−25 °C, not −40 °C.** Narrower than most parts in this catalog. [1]
- **No stability data is published.** A 431 driving an optocoupler is a real
  control loop, and this datasheet contains no capacitive-load stability region
  or gain-phase plot to design it with. Copying a proven reference design, or
  testing the loop, is wiser than improvising. [1]
